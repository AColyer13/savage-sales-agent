# 04 Demo, Mutual Action Plan, Negotiation

## Killer demo
**Before the demo, know**: their current state; what's wrong with what they do today; how it looks today; their goals.
**5 slides**: Goals & Priorities / Current Challenges / What they're looking for / How we can help / What we're going to show.
**Rules**: identify the spoon (if they want a spoon, don't sell the whole kitchen set). Customize to paint the picture. Do a value check (show exactly how it solves their problem). Know their industry and buzzwords. KISS: less is more; focus on them and their problems; tell them what you'll show; show it; pause; confirm it's what they're looking for.
**60-minute run of show**: 5 buffer / 10 slides / 20 demo / 15 pricing / 10 next steps.
**Closing**: capitalize on momentum; get cards on the table ("do you think this would solve your problems?"; surface concerns now); don't leave empty-handed, drive a black-and-white next step with the next call scheduled.
**Pro tips**: use your SE as the Trojan horse ("eat crow": let them confirm technical points; as the AE don't be the expert people are skeptical of); practice with the SE; make it frictionless (use their conferencing tool); early meetings.

## First-meeting talk track (When to position: at the end of / after the first meeting)
"My job is to help you and your team make an educated decision as quickly and effectively as possible. How does tomorrow look to walk through how we'll do just that, and to also get aligned on what steps your organization typically takes to evaluate technology? Who else on your side is needed on that call to ensure we can get all of the steps outlined in detail so we don't miss anything?"

## Mutual Action Plan (MAP)
Purpose statement: "This document is used to provide transparent, efficient, and collaborative two-way communication to assist [Prospect Company] with their evaluation of [Seller Company] as they work toward achieving [unique goal] by [ideal date]."
Fields: start date, estimated completion, buyer name/team, your company's key stakeholders, then 16 numbered steps with owner (buyer/seller), due date, and status. Build it WITH the buyer (no surprises), back-plan from their ideal date, and include their internal steps (security, legal, procurement, budget approval, exec sign-off).
Typical steps to draft: kickoff and goals, discovery with each persona, demo/trial, success criteria agreed, stakeholder alignment, business case review, pricing/proposal, legal/security/procurement, executive sign-off, contract, implementation kickoff.

## Negotiation lifecycle (5 stages)
1. **Decide**: identify the issue, define the problem, decide to negotiate, demand, or agree.
2. **Prepare**: understand the problem; define mutual goals; communicate levers; build relationships; people/roles; define your best/worst backup options and theirs.
3. **Negotiate**: present alternatives; evaluate them; decide on the best path; get agreements in writing; create/adjust action plan and timeline.
4. **Execute**: congratulate everyone involved; follow up to implement the action plan.
5. **Follow up**: ensure implementation is on track; help as needed; nurture relationships.
