# 09 SUPPLEMENT: Champions, negotiation, forecasting, battlecards, renewals (NOT from the course)
Source: general practice (see SOURCES.md). Mostly practitioner and vendor guidance, not research; where evidence is thin it says so.
Where this meets the course, the course wins.

## Champion test (extends 06-business-case.md)
Course test: access to power, skin in the game, proven they're in. Practitioner addition: **test with an action**. Ask them to do something
(set up a meeting with the economic buyer, share internal evaluation criteria, present to the buying committee). If they won't act, they're a **coach, not a champion**.
Keep them separate from the **economic buyer** (final budget authority): the champion builds momentum, the economic buyer approves. Ask the champion to confirm who that is and to introduce you.
Before presenting, get a short preliminary conversation with the economic buyer so the case uses the metrics they already track.

## Multi-threading
Never rely on one contact. Map stakeholders (influence, skeptic/neutral/fan) as in the QBR template; have at least one live relationship per key persona.
A deal with a single contact is a Deal Inspection red flag (Why Us #20, Why Anything #10).

## Negotiation: trade, don't discount (extends the course's Negotiation Lifecycle)
- Know your **walk-away** point before you start (Prepare stage: best/worst options for both sides).
- **Give-Get**: never give a concession for free. Pair each give with a specific get. Script: "If you're willing to [GET], then I'm able to [GIVE]."
  Examples of gets: longer term, prepayment, case study/reference, faster signature, wider scope, intro to the economic buyer.
- Concessions should be small, slow, conditional, and written down. Avoid the unilateral give, splitting the difference, large fast jumps, and unwritten gets.
- Keep a two-column ledger of gives and gets for each negotiation. A discount repeats at every renewal, so price it as recurring.
- Get agreements in writing (course step) and update the MAP.

## Pipeline hygiene and forecasting (course covers territory/quota, not forecasting)
- Every stage needs a **verifiable exit criterion** (something the buyer did, not something you did). 5-7 stages is typical.
- Forecast as a **range**: Commit (high confidence: e.g., signed or agreed proposal, confirmed procurement timeline, a dated next step), Best Case (commit plus realistic upside), Worst Case.
- **Coverage**: roughly 3-4x pipeline to quota is the common rule of thumb; calibrate from your own win rate and cycle. Coverage lies if pipeline is stale or single-threaded, so run Deal Inspection on the big ones.
- Smaller, accurate pipeline beats a large, inflated one. Kill dead deals ("time kills all deals"). Don't inflate probabilities.
- Your own funnel (course PCV): call-to-connect, connect-to-demo, demo-to-trial/proposal, close rate. Fix the weakest step first (DMAIC).

## Competitive battlecard (one page)
Sections: Why we win (3 specific deal conditions, honest) / Where we lose and why / Landmine questions to plant early (expose their weakness, e.g. "how long did implementation take for their last three customers?") /
Their likely objections about us and responses / Killer questions / Proof (real). Build from win-loss data and call notes, not opinion. Refresh quarterly or when a competitor changes pricing/features.
Pairs with the cold-call objection "We use [competitor]": love / change / why that?

## Renewals and expansion (course touches it via Customer Journey stages 4-5 and "Existing Customer Base Strategy")
Retention stage: progress made, create a fan. Schedule value reviews against the success criteria set in the MAP. Ask the "Why Anything/Us/Now" again at renewal (what changed). Look for new personas and use cases (seed and grow).
Do not invent ROI; use the customer's own measured results.
