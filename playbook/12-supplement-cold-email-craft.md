# 12 SUPPLEMENT: Cold email craft, cadence and testing (NOT from the course)
Source: ideas synthesized from published work by Joanna Wiebe (Copyhackers), Will Allred (Lavender), Chad S. White, Flint McGlaughlin (MECLABS) and Jeb Blount (Sales Gravy).
Statistics those authors cite (reply-rate lifts, send-time effects) are vendor/author claims, unverified here: treat as hypotheses to test, never as facts to quote to a buyer.
Where this conflicts with the course (playbooks 01-07, 10), the course wins. Style stays Savage: pain before pitch, curious not salesy, always a next step.

## Hard rule for every template here
Numbers, percentages, hours, peer companies and results are ALWAYS placeholders: `[ADD METRIC: source]`, `[ADD PEER: real customer]`. Fill them only from `profile.md` or the user. If there is no real proof, drop the proof line; do not invent it.

## 1. Write for one reader
One reader, one idea, one ask. Run a "so what?" test on every sentence: if it states a feature, rewrite it as time saved, money gained, or risk avoided that the reader already cares about. Cut anything that fails.

## 2. Match the awareness stage
| Stage | The reader... | Lead with |
|---|---|---|
| Unaware | doesn't see a problem | a specific observation about their world; no product |
| Problem-aware | feels the pain, no solution in mind | the pain in their words, and what it costs to leave it |
| Solution-aware | knows fixes exist | how your approach differs |
| Product-aware | knows you | proof and a low-risk next step |
Default for cold outreach is unaware or problem-aware. Never pitch features to someone who barely recognizes the problem (this is the course's "no pain, no pitch").

## 3. Use customer words (voice of customer)
Use phrases the buyer uses, taken from real reviews, support tickets, call notes or posts the user supplies (see `/voc`). Quote only text actually provided; mark source. Never fabricate a "typical" quote.

## 4. Mobile-first form (directional guidance)
- Short lines, one idea per line, plain words (aim for a reading level a 10-year-old follows), about 50-100 words for a first touch.
- Subject: 1-4 words, lowercase or plain, no punctuation or hype, something a colleague could write. It must match what the email delivers.
- The first ~40-90 characters of body also show as preview text: do not waste them on "Hope you're well".
- Tentative tone can disarm ("might be off base", "not sure if this is on your radar"). Test it; do not assume it works for every buyer.
- First ask is an interest-ask ("open to seeing how X handled it?"), not a time-ask ("15 minutes Tuesday?"). Booking the dated next step comes once they respond (course rule: every deal ends with a dated next step).

## 5. Lower anxiety at the ask
Friction is effort; anxiety is fear of a pushy call. Next to the ask, remove the fear: "happy to send a one-page summary by email first, no call needed" (only if you will actually do it). Make the email's job one reply or click, not the whole sale.

## 6. Three structure templates (all numbers are placeholders)
**A. Operational bottleneck**
```
Subject: [prospect company] [process]
Hi [First Name],
Noticed [ADD: real, researched trigger about their team].
Teams like yours often spend [ADD METRIC: source] on [specific process] instead of [higher-value work].
[ADD PEER: real customer] [ADD RESULT: from profile.md].
Open to seeing how they set it up?
[Your name]
```
**B. Hidden leak (tentative)**
```
Subject: question on [specific metric]
Hi [First Name],
Might be off base, but [role plural] I talk to tell me [ADD: pain, in their words, from /voc].
Catching it late tends to cost more than catching it early. [ADD METRIC if you have a real one, otherwise delete this line]
Worth a quick look at how [ADD PEER: real customer] handled it?
[Your name]
```
**C. Skeptical buyer (anxiety removal)**
```
Subject: [process] overhead
Hi [First Name],
Keeping [manual step] in-house usually means ongoing maintenance on [what].
[ADD PEER: real customer] [ADD RESULT: from profile.md] without replacing their current setup. [Delete if untrue]
Happy to send a one-page breakdown by email first. No call needed. Sound fair?
[Your name]
```

## 7. Cadence (optional supplement; the course calendar in playbook 10 wins)
Cluster a call, a short voicemail and the email within 24-48 hours so the name is familiar. The voicemail names the email's subject so the email feels expected.
Example 21-day shape: day 1 call+voicemail then email; day 3 call; day 4 threaded 25-word follow-up adding ONE new thing; day 7 call+voicemail; day 8 new-angle email; day 11 call; day 12 follow-up offering a no-call asset; day 15 call+voicemail; day 17 new-angle email; day 21 call plus a short permission-to-close note.
Batch like with like (dial block, then email block) to avoid context switching. Windows (morning dials, afternoon emails, early/late sends) are things to test, not facts.
Voicemail script (about 15 seconds): "Hi [First Name], it's [Name]. No need to call back. I'm sending a short note today with the subject '[subject]' about [ADD: their pain]. Keep an eye out for it."

## 8. Testing without fooling yourself
- Test one big thing at a time before small ones: pain angle vs proof angle, plain text vs formatted, interest-ask vs no-call asset. Subject-line wording last.
- Judge by positive reply rate or booked meetings, never opens (open tracking is unreliable).
- Cold reply rates are low, so small samples are noise. Example: 125 sends at a 3% reply rate is about 4 replies, so a "25% lift" is one reply. Run each variant to a large enough sample (hundreds each; see playbook 08) before declaring a winner, and treat a small test as a hint only.
- A 2x2 (framing x ask) is fine only if volume supports four cells; otherwise test one variable at a time.
- Keep the winner as the new control, then change the next variable.
