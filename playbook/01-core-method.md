# 01 Core Method

## What sales is (First Day)
Not a transaction; you are helping people. Be curious. Help make other people's lives better. Ask "how can I help
more people?" Control your own destiny. Foundation trackers: Clarity, Purpose, Gratitude, Cost of Inaction, Calendar.

## The 3 Whys (the spine of every deal)
- **Why Anything**: why do they need to change at all? The problem/opportunity they want to address. (They may not know they have a problem.)
- **Why Us**: why us over the alternative? Differentiation and value, **for that persona**.
- **Why Now**: why is there urgency? Without it, deals stall. Time kills all deals.

## FALL question method
**F**rame, **A**sk, **L**isten, **L**ayer.
- Frame template: "Hey [name], every time I talk with a [role] in [year], because [a] they're doing [b]. So from your perspective, how are you thinking about [b] for your team?"
- Layering = follow-up curiosity: "Blue." -> "Oh interesting, why blue?" Be *interested*, not *interesting*.
- Get priorities, budget, and who else needs to be involved. Don't turn it into an interrogation; give value as you get value.

## Airtight meeting: 5-part framework
1. **Frame**: align on Purpose (why are we here), Plan (what we will and won't cover), Outcome (the destination), and get Agreement.
2. **Behavior**: 70% of communication isn't the words. Detached from outcome? Seeking to understand first? Genuinely curious? Smiling, conversational?
3. **Execute**: right questions the right way; come with a point of view; align on pain, priorities, solution; qualify; use customer stories.
4. **Objections**: pause, find the real objection, don't get defensive.
5. **Next Steps**: block time; get feedback on what was accomplished first; schedule the next step before getting off the call.
Goals: buyer and seller work together, more gets done, urgency created and kept, better buying experience (arguably #1).

## Evaluating prospects ("first date")
- Meet them where they are in the buying process. Don't ask about marriage on a first date.
- Ask what would make this meeting a 10/10, then deliver it (this alone builds rapport).
- Get clear on pain beyond the surface. Connect the dots through story. Make them feel they came to the right place.
- **Make it easy**: changing an organization is a lot of work. Show how simple the evaluation is, lay out the steps, make the leap of faith easy.
- You are the expert (leverage an SE if there is one; "eat crow": let the SE confirm technical points).
- Never leave the other party empty-handed; never leave without a next step on the calendar; get their cell (don't blow it up).

## Mindset checklist (also scored in the call grader)
Pause before responding. No assumptions. Detached from closing. Balanced give and take. Use their language and goals.
Keep control without being pushy. Slow down. Calm, casual, concise.
